The Multan Solar Market in 2026

Multan’s solar market grew out of its electronics and hardware trade, and it shows. The city has some of the most competitive equipment retail pricing in Pakistan — panel and inverter dealers around Hussain Agahi and the Vehari Road corridor move serious volume — but engineering-led installation is far scarcer than in Lahore or the twin cities. The result is a market where hardware is cheap, quotes look attractive, and the difference between a properly designed system and a dealer’s turnkey kit only becomes visible in the first summer’s generation numbers.

MEPCO serves Multan under NEPRA’s 2026 Prosumer Regulations (net billing), covering the geographically largest DISCO territory in Pakistan. That breadth is also its weakness: application volumes from thirteen districts funnel through the same approval machinery, and a complete file from Multan city typically takes 7–12 weeks through to bidirectional meter installation — slower than LESCO or IESCO. An installer who knows the MEPCO Multan circle offices and pre-empts the inspection checklist saves weeks.

Heat is the defining engineering constraint. Multan is consistently among Pakistan’s hottest major cities, with June peaks above 45°C and roof-surface temperatures far higher. Panel output falls as cell temperature rises, so the temperature coefficient on the datasheet matters more here than almost anywhere in the country. N-type TOPCon panels lose noticeably less output per degree than older P-type PERC stock still common in the dealer market, and a mounting design with a proper ventilation gap can recover several percent of summer yield. A Multan quote that doesn’t discuss heat derating is a red flag.

Dust is the second constraint. Pre-monsoon dust storms roll through South Punjab from May to July, and cotton-season lint adds to soiling around the ginning belt. Panels in Multan need cleaning every 3–4 weeks in summer — roughly twice as often as Islamabad — or generation quietly slides 8–15 percent. Ask every installer what cleaning access their mounting design allows.

Residential demand concentrates in DHA Multan, Wapda Town, Gulgasht Colony, Model Town, Shah Rukn-e-Alam Colony, New Multan, and the Bosan Road corridor, with Cantt properties adding an approval layer. Commercial work clusters in the Multan Industrial Estate and along Vehari Road, and South Punjab’s agricultural economy adds a segment most cities don’t have: solar tubewell conversions and cold-storage loads for the mango and citrus trade, which reward installers who can size pumps and motors, not just rooftops.

The companies ranked below mix national engineering firms with Multan’s strongest local operators. Locals win on price and parts availability; nationals win on design depth and monitoring. The ranking weighs both.

Multan-specific FAQs

How long does MEPCO net metering take in Multan?

MEPCO processes net-metering applications under NEPRA’s 2026 Prosumer Regulations. A complete file from Multan city typically clears in 7–12 weeks through to bidirectional meter installation — slower than LESCO or IESCO because thirteen districts share the same approval pipeline. Incomplete files routinely lose a month, so in-house filing by your installer matters.

Does Multan’s heat reduce solar output?

Yes, measurably. Panel output drops as cell temperature rises, and Multan’s 45°C+ summers push cell temperatures past 70°C on poorly ventilated roofs. N-type TOPCon panels with low temperature coefficients and a mounting gap for airflow limit the loss to a few percent; cheap P-type stock flat-mounted to the roof can lose 12–18 percent at peak. This is the single biggest quality gap between Multan installers.

Is solar worth it for tubewells around Multan?

Usually, yes. A diesel or grid-fed tubewell in the mango and cotton belt typically pays back a properly sized solar conversion in 2–4 seasons. The engineering is different from rooftop work — motor sizing, VFD selection, and seasonal water-table variation all matter — so use an installer with actual agri references, not just rooftop experience.

How to Choose the Right Solar Company in Multan

  • • Demand a heat-aware design. Ask for the panel’s temperature coefficient and the mounting ventilation gap in writing. In Multan’s summers, this single specification separates systems that deliver from systems that disappoint.
  • • Confirm MEPCO net metering handling. MEPCO is slower than LESCO or IESCO, and incomplete files lose a month. Confirm the company files end-to-end in-house, including inspection coordination at the Multan circle.
  • • Get a cleaning plan, not just a system. Dust storms and cotton lint mean panels need cleaning every 3–4 weeks in summer. Ask how the mounting design allows access and whether the company offers a cleaning contract.
  • • Compare the bill of materials, not the headline price. Multan’s dealer market produces very cheap quotes. The difference is usually P-type panels, undersized cabling, or no monitoring — invisible on the quote, expensive on the bill.
  • • Insist on monitoring. Heat derating and soiling losses are gradual and invisible without real-time data. Ask which platform is included and whether it sends fault alerts.
  • • Match the installer to the job. Rooftop, industrial, and tubewell work are different disciplines. Ask for references for your specific project type — DHA rooftop, Industrial Estate shed, or orchard tubewell.
