Why Go Solar in Hyderabad?

Hyderabad sits at the centre of Sindh’s commercial belt, with cotton ginning, agriculture, light industry, and a large residential base all drawing heavily on the HESCO grid. Summer temperatures regularly exceed 45°C and HESCO load shedding remains a daily reality across most residential feeders. The city receives approximately 3,100 hours of sunshine per year — among the highest in Pakistan — making solar both economically attractive and operationally essential.

HESCO net billing lets Hyderabad consumers export surplus generation, bought back at roughly Rs 10-11 per unit under NEPRA’s 2026 Prosumer Regulations. For a Latifabad or Qasimabad household running heavy AC and refrigeration loads, a 10-15kW system typically cuts a PKR 60,000-90,000 bill by PKR 40,000-75,000. Commercial sites in Saddar and on the National Highway corridor see even faster paybacks given peak-hour tariff exposure.

Solar Citizen engineers Hyderabad systems for the city’s specific conditions — marine-corridor humidity, dust from the desert side, and the high-heat operating envelope of South Sindh. We use anodized aluminium racking, IP66-rated string inverters, and panel selections chosen for low-temperature-coefficient performance. SOL AI monitoring tracks soiling, heat derating, and string-level faults with rupee-value loss estimates — three of the most common Sindh-side cost leaks.

HESCO Net Metering: How It Works

HESCO supplies electricity across most of South Sindh including Hyderabad, Tando Allahyar, Mirpurkhas, Matiari, Tando Muhammad Khan, Badin, Thatta, and Sujawal. Since February 2026, new applicants come under NEPRA’s Prosumer Regulations (SRO 251(I)/2026): the surplus you export to HESCO is bought at roughly Rs 10-11 per unit under net billing, while the units you import are billed at your normal tariff. The old 1:1 net-metering deal continues only for systems approved before February 2026, until their agreements expire. The maths now rewards self-consumption — size the system to what you use during the day, and treat exports as a bonus.

The process Solar Citizen handles for every Hyderabad customer:

  1. Bidirectional meter application filed with HESCO on your behalf
  2. AEDB (Alternative Energy Development Board) registration as a prosumer
  3. Site inspection scheduled with HESCO’s technical team
  4. Bidirectional meter installed, replacing your existing unidirectional meter
  5. Net metering agreement signed and grid synchronization activated

Typical timeline: 6 – 10 weeks from system commissioning to net metering activation, depending on HESCO scheduling and AEDB processing. Solar Citizen handles all paperwork and follow-up — including agricultural-tubewell net metering for South Sindh farmers and ginning-mill commercial filings, both of which differ slightly from standard residential.
